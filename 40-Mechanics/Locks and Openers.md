# Locks and Openers

How things open in №121. Ratified in Round 12.

## The two rules

1. **No traditional keys, and no keyholes as a default.** A lock in this game is rarely a lock: it is smoke, a chain without a keyhole, a door that will not answer, a lid, a seam, a wall with a hole in it.
2. **A signed certificate never unlocks anything.** Certification closes a work-order line and fixes a measure under [[Clause 02 — The Measure]]. It is paperwork. See [[The Ruler]].

## What openers look like

Diverse, physical, and diegetic. Each is an object or an act with a visible mechanism — something to crank, wind, pour, align, play, or shine. Chests, cans, an elevator, a skeleton cranked open: the shape of the lock should tell the player what kind of act it wants, without a written prompt.

## The afterimage

RMB flash burns a shape onto the screen in **screen space**, not world space. The player walks; it stays where it is on the monitor; they line it up with a matching hole or seam elsewhere and the passage opens.

**The decay is timer-based** — ratified in Round 12, and the reason the *zero timers anywhere* rule in [[The Day Loop]] was retired. It is the one place in the game where elapsed time presses on the player, because the mechanic is literally about an image fading.

🔧 Duration unset. It should be long enough to cross several screens and short enough that dithering costs the attempt.
