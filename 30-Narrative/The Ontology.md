# The Ontology

> [!warning] Top-secret layer. Never stated plainly in-game; assembled by the player from evidence, and confirmed only at the endings. The authored misreading is [[The Tomb Reading]].

## What the world is

№121 **is** [[Anna]] — the shape her last consciousness took as it faded. Not a world she built and left behind; the thing she became.

Still **not** a computer simulation. The banned-technique list in [[The Two-Grammar Rule]] holds and hardens: wrongness is analog, architectural and bureaucratic because a dying mind furnishing itself out of memory has no other vocabulary.

## The lifeboat, misread as a tomb

Her intent was **survival**. She died in 1998 — see [[Timeline]] and [[The Death of Anna]] — and what remained of her took shape as a place that could hold people, because an apartment building is the most ordinary machine there is for keeping people who would otherwise disperse.

The player is meant to read it as a **grave**. Evidence is planted that way on purpose; see [[The Tomb Reading]]. The tomb conclusion is fully derivable on [[The Corkboard Thread]], and it is wrong.

## Why the Building fades

Every scope lock in the [[Scope Ledger]] now has a fictional cause. She can hold roughly thirty rooms. [[The Sealed Fifth Floor]] is a floor she has already lost. The sixth floor that isn't there is where the loss shows, and [[Room 696]] is what moved into it. The "no vacancies" policy is her holding on with both hands.

[[Mr. Vacancy]] **existed before she died** and took her family one at a time — see [[The Curse]]. Inside the vessel it is additionally fed by three sources: entropy caused the birth of the dying wish, the dying wish strengthens as she loses hope of being answered, and [[Otto]]'s guilt and heartbreak mix into it.

**The Building is mind and body both.** Each floor corresponds to an organ, and anomalies are generated from it — see [[The Organ Map]] and [[The Body]].

## The two accounts of leaving

- **Anchor physics (the truth):** the vessel runs on [[Otto]]'s habitation and participation. Keeping the Building intact keeps her last piece alive, and keeps open the chance of bringing her back.
- **Decommission (the claim):** the Administration threatens "consolidation" in perfect legalese.

The player can never cleanly verify which account is true from inside; evidence is planted both ways. The difference under this ontology is that **both accounts are hers** — the Administration is her own institutional register, not a third party.

## Why Otto

By her choice. She called him. The rival explanations are authored misdirection and stay findable all game:

- **Blood and origin** — everything here feels familiar to him because her memory of the place contains him. See [[Timeline]].
- **The post** — [[Renner]] held it before him, so the post looks like the anchor rather than the man.
- **Attention** — everyone else who believed she was still alive is now dead, so he looks like the last one left to call.

Only he can repair her and fight the corruption. The other tenants can maintain the Building; none of them can fight back. See [[Otto]].

**Settled in Round 4:** the five years are three clocks completing at once — the body's conversion, the accumulation of Otto's search, and the declaration of death in absentia. **Anna does not get stronger**; she reaches for him every night and a lost soul is weak. What changes in 2003 is Otto's ability to sense her, boosted by heartbreak on the day of the declaration. See [[Timeline]].

## The Administration

**Paper-only** (revised Round 7). It is notices and the tenancy agreement behind the House Rules, and nothing more. It does not appear, does not explain, and does not assert itself as a character; it surfaces only where a beat explicitly calls for it.

What it does is triage rather than tyranny: sealed floors and midnight hammering are patching, misread as menace. Work crews are only ever heard through walls. Management is never seen.

The Building has intent and no vocabulary. It can issue a work order, grow a room, seal a floor and walk you counter-clockwise; it cannot say *stay*. This is why the House Rules read as an ancient tenancy agreement — legalese is the only language she has left.
